# Playtest #1 — Recruiting & Logistics Kit

Turnkey materials to line up the 4–6 kids for Playtest #1. Pair with `playtest-1-plan.md`
(the protocol). This is the "how to actually get kids in chairs" companion.

---

## 1. Who we need (screener)

Aim for **4–6 kids**. You learn the big things in the first 3; 6 gives a safety margin.

**Include if:**
- Age **~6–9**.
- Has **dyslexia and/or ADHD** (diagnosed or in-eval is fine — a parent saying "reading is hard for them" counts).
- Reading **at or below** grade level.
- A **parent/guardian can be present** for ~20 min.

**Aim for a mix:** at least one clearly-dyslexia profile and one clearly-ADHD profile if you can — they stress different parts of the design.

**Skip if:**
- Strong reader well above level (not our audience; won't surface the real signals).
- Child can't sit for ~10 min even with breaks (reschedule, don't force).

**Where to find them:** your own network first (fastest), then — reading tutors / SLPs, a
local dyslexia-support group or Facebook parent group, a pediatric OT/education clinic,
a homeschool co-op, or a teacher who works with struggling readers. Specialists are gold:
they know exactly which kids fit and parents trust their referral.

## 2. Recruitment message (copy-paste, adapt the blanks)

> **Hi [name] — quick favor / opportunity.** I'm working on a small iPhone reading game
> designed *for* kids who find reading hard — specifically kids with dyslexia or ADHD.
> Before we build it out, I want to watch a few real kids play the early version for about
> 15 minutes, so we build the right thing.
>
> Would [child's name] be up for trying it while you sit with them? It's **play, not a
> test** — no scores shown to them, nothing to study, no wrong answers. I'll be watching to
> learn what works, not evaluating your child. **[In-person at ___ / over a quick video call],
> about 15–20 minutes, [date options].** Totally fine to stop anytime.
>
> As a thank-you, [small gift card / bookstore voucher / nothing — your call]. Interested?

*Note: keep "no scores, no test, no wrong answers" in — it's true (we designed for it) and
it's exactly what an anxious parent of a struggling reader needs to hear.*

## 3. Consent (short, plain-language)

Get a **yes in writing** (text/email is fine for an informal test) covering:
- The child will play an early app for ~15 min while you observe.
- You'll take **written notes only** — **no photo/video/audio of the child** unless the
  parent separately, explicitly says yes.
- Notes are **de-identified** (first name or initials only).
- You can **stop anytime**, and the data won't be used beyond improving the app.

> "I'm OK with [child's initials] trying the app for ~15 min while I'm there. Notes only,
> no recording. — [parent name], [date]"

That line back over text is enough for an informal playtest.

## 4. Logistics checklist

**Before:**
- [ ] Device ready with the current build installed (simulator on a laptop is OK; a real
      iPhone in the kid's hands is better). Confirm it launches to the game screen.
- [ ] **Fresh start per kid** — each child should begin with no prior progress (see the note
      in `playtest-1-plan.md`; if the build carries state between kids, reset between sessions).
- [ ] Print or open the **observation rubric** (playtest-1-plan §5), one per kid.
- [ ] Quiet-ish space, minimal distractions (matters for the ADHD kids).
- [ ] Optional small thank-you gift.

**During:** follow the protocol (playtest-1-plan §4). Hand it over with almost no instruction;
**stay quiet**; watch the **first miss** closely; note everything.

**After each kid:** fill the rubric within 5 minutes while it's fresh. If the build shows a
grown-up recap, glance at it for accuracy/latency — but weight what you *saw* over the numbers.

## 5. Schedule

- **15–20 min per child**, back-to-back is fine but leave 5 min between to write notes and
  reset the device.
- 4–6 kids ≈ **half a day**, or spread across a few days.
- Don't batch more than ~4 in one sitting — your observation quality drops.

## 6. Right after: the readout

Within a day, pull the rubrics together and answer the three questions from
`playtest-1-plan §1`: understood unaided? failure safe? worth returning to? Then apply the
GO / REWORK decision (§6). Loop me (Fellow) in with the filled rubrics and I'll help read the
signal and decide next steps.
